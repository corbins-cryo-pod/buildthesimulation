What this experiment calculates

A reconstructed human cell

Allen specimen 571654895, a human middle temporal gyrus layer-2 spiny cell, with published fitted active membrane parameters. The 40 µm mesh has 211 membrane compartments and 75 zero-area junctions. A 20 µm mesh is available for refinement.

Dendritic geometry comes from the source reconstruction. The active axon is the source-prescribed 60 µm synthetic stub. The morphology does not establish a full biological axon or its initiation site.

Allen / Mosher–Wei model source ↗

Light-sensitive membrane current

ChR2(H134R) follows the Williams et al. four-state model with its activation variable, voltage dependence, temperature factors, and current–voltage fit. Each expressing membrane compartment has its own state. Conductance density is an assumed experimental input, not measured expression in this specimen.

The source fit was characterized at approximately −80 to −10 mV. This cell rests near −92 mV after conditioning and crosses positive voltages during a spike, so both regions use the mathematical extrapolation. All neuron experiments use 34 °C, matching the native channel model.

Williams et al., 2013 ↗

A computed optical field

Uniform illumination provides a controlled local-exposure experiment. The fiber uses a seeded Monte Carlo field for a 100 µm core, NA 0.22, 470 nm source in homogeneous bulk tissue. Power scales the field; its orientation and position determine compartment exposure.

There is no tissue surface, implant obstruction, blood-vessel geometry, or thermal solver. The displayed cone indicates source direction; the light overlay samples the computed field.

OptogenSIM optical-property reference ↗

Computed traces and reproducibility

Native membrane channels and opsin current enter the compartmental cable solve. The neuron conditions for 100 ms before the displayed experiment. Somatic events are detected at the solver timestep; the replay uses saved samples.

Each export includes the full experiment configuration. The CSV contains computed traces and local exposure; it is not an experimental recording. Channel assay is one ideal isopotential membrane patch, not a perfect voltage clamp of the branched cell.

Verification ledger

Optical quantity and calibration boundary

The fiber kernel is scalar fluence rate, integrated over photon directions, in mW/mm² per mW emitted. It is not directional irradiance at a flat detector. Uniform light is a prescribed local exposure. Passing a diffuse fluence rate to an opsin model characterized with irradiance assumes a matching effective photon exposure; that calibration is not established for this human specimen.

The original 1.3 photon-loss factor in the Williams fit is retained even with tissue attenuation. Its transfer from channel characterization to tissue fluence is an explicit coupling assumption, not a measured tissue correction. Optical energy and integrated fluence are not tissue temperature or safety estimates.

The current fit is evaluated as ḡ(O₁ + 0.1 O₂)[10.6408 − 14.6408 exp(V / −42.7671)]. This removes the division by V in the published form. The fitted current is not forced to zero at V = 0; positive-voltage and resting-voltage use is extrapolation.

This is a computational research and learning model. Numerical checks and source attribution are distinct from experimental validation of the combined optical–neuron prediction. Chloride inhibition, other opsins, connected networks, and heating remain separate extensions.
