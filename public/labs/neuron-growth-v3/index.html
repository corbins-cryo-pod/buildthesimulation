What changed in v3
  • Axon identity is revocable. A sibling that out-grows the axon by 12 µm for 150 min takes over. Cutting an axon clears its label, so the longest remaining neurite becomes the new axon (Cut axon button, Alt-click, or the Axon transection scenario).
  • Stages follow growth. Dendrites start once the axon is long enough and spines once the dendrites are, instead of on fixed timers. Stage 3 is dated from the start of the winning neurite's lead, as time-lapse studies date it.
  • Calibration, not validation. Approximate, author-selected targets inspired by Dotti et al. 1988: axon about 19 h, slow dendrites (≈12 µm a day each) and an axon several times longer than any dendrite.
  • Death is a hazard. Starved neurons die at random, sooner the deeper the deficit, instead of in one timed wave.
  • Glia act at a distance. Astrocytes support nearby neurons through a diffusible factor, and Feeder layer adds uniform support as in the Banker sandwich culture.
  • Stiffness matters. A slipping clutch wastes the tip's tubulin, so DRG axons grow longest at intermediate stiffness.
  • Step-size consistent. Mechanics now advance in simulated time, so changing the time step no longer changes neurite lengths.
  • Turning assay. The pipette sits about 45° off the axons' path, as in Ming et al. 1997, so turning can be measured against each axon's own heading.
From v2
  • Supply. The soma makes tubulin at a fixed rate; tips draw on it through diffusion plus kinesin transport that waves boost. Neurites compete, so one wins and becomes the axon.
  • Identity. Minor neurites become an axon or dendrites (lilac and green). Stages 1 to 5 are tracked per cell and compared with the classic culture timeline.
  • Cell types. Each cell reads a genome: layout of primaries, speeds, branching mode, self-avoidance, tiling, migration and firing class.
  • Clutch. Cone traction and retrograde flow come from a precomputed motor-clutch model, so stiffness and coating change growth.
  • Coverslip. Coatings, stiffness maps, PDMS walls, cue fields with noisy gradient sensing, radial-glia fibers.
  • Life cycle. Trophic support, apoptosis, dividing astrocytes, neurogenesis and migration modes.
  • Activity. Spiking neurons, synaptic weights with STDP and depression, calcium-stalled cones and homeostatic synapse turnover.
  • Tools. Sholl profile, stage timing, SWC export; the engine runs in a background worker.
