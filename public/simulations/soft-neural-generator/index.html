The engine behind the membrane

This is a new independent mechanics prototype. It is not the earlier fixed-centerline transport demonstration and does not replace the deployed Cell Growth Lab.

1 / Softness without losing the cell

A 48-site deformable cortical ring encloses an oblate soma. Adhesive spreading competes with effective cortical tension and area elasticity. A finite nucleus supplies a minimum soma-height constraint. Structural outgrowth withdraws volume from the body approximation.

V_body = V_initial − π r_shaft² Σ L_material h = 3 V_body / (2 A_projected) E_spread(A) = K(A/A₀ − 1)² A₀/2 + σ max(0,A/A₀ − 1)² A₀/2 − W A + E_nucleus Overdamped radial motion + local actin forces + cortical smoothing

Bookkeeping conserves a reduced body + shaft volume proxy. The rendered irregular skin, widening waves and filopodia are not independently volume-integrated. Membrane area/exocytosis are not yet conserved.

2 / Flexible internal structure, temporary grip

Each neurite is an extensible, bending chain. Its root shares forces with the soma or its parent process. Actomyosin recovery shortens the mechanical rest target, while lasting growth adds structural rest length from a finite transported supply. Tip fingers sweep, elongate, catastrophize, bind, load, and release. Each is covered by the same continuous cell mask.

ζ dx/dt = F_stretch + F_bend + F_adhesion + F_contact F_stretch ∝ L_geometric − L_material(1 − active_contraction) k_off(load) = k_release [k₀ + k_slip(exp(load/load₀) − 1)] M_total = M_soma + Σ M_in_transit + Σ L_material = constant

Microtubules do not stick through the plasma membrane. The fingers represent actin-rich membrane protrusions with a coarse internal microtubule-invasion fraction. Gold lines visualize the flexible coarse scaffold, not resolved microtubules.

3 / Local waves rather than moving graphical packets

Each scaffold interval has an excitable activator and a slower recovery variable. Asymmetric nearest-neighbor coupling gives anterograde propagation. Ignition is stochastic and locally refractory; a user pulse is an explicit experimental intervention. Width, microtubule support, cargo flow, and growth respond locally to the computed field.

da/dt = r a(1−a)(a−θ) − β h a + D ∇²_biased a + stimulus dh/dt = k_on a − k_off h MT support: local actin-dependent gain + turnover Growth: local cone activity × support × finite tip supply

These are phenomenological excitable equations, not identified actin biochemistry. The soma-initiated bias is a modeling assumption. The coefficients were not estimated from the paper.

4 / Branching and population scaling

New primary processes require a persistent cortical hotspot. Lateral branches require retained exploratory protrusions, internal support, and a stochastic stabilization step. No neuron shape is pre-drawn. A spatial hash restricts contact searches. Neurite crossings remain separate graph edges; this 2.5-D implementation does not resolve physical 3-D overpasses, full membrane collisions, synapses, axon identity, or electrical activity.

Evidence and scope

Winans et al. (2016) supports coupled actin waves, transient shaft widening, microtubule polymerization, transport, and intermittent early neurite extension. Flynn et al. (2009) reports growth-cone-like waves and branch-associated shaft protrusions. Bard et al. (2008) and Nichol et al. (2016) support adhesion–actin coupling and traction. Li et al. (2015) provides a non-neuronal mechanical spreading reference; it is not a neuronal calibration.

A contrasting 2017 study found wave-associated retraction and exploration without increased net elongation in its cultures. This engine therefore tests a wave-coupled growth hypothesis, not a universal rule that every actin wave must lengthen a neurite.

Verification is not biological validation. Neither individual microtubules, membrane chemistry, realistic material constants, molecular cargo species, nor developmental cell identity are resolved. Whole-cell crawling remains weak; tip growth is not proof of soma locomotion. See the model and deployment notes.
