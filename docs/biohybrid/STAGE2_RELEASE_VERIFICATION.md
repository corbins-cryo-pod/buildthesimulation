## Causal audit and source attribution

The default audit uses two graft cells, three hosts, a 1,000 µm tract, 25 µm segment target, 0.005 ms integration step, 40 ms duration, and two −24 µA stimulus pulses with 0.4 ms phases. The model contains 574 active membrane compartments and 82 massless junction nodes. Matched synaptic block sets only the host–graft synaptic weight to zero; extracellular stimulation remains applied to both populations. The no-stimulus control sets amplitude to zero.

| Default condition | Hosts with soma spikes | Total soma spikes, graft + host | R2 neural / µV p–p | R2 graft / µV p–p | R2 host / µV p–p |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Intact | 3/3 | 10 | 9.74532994 | 5.47945072 | 5.92722510 |
| Synapses off | 0/3 | 4 | 5.47945203 | 5.47945072 | 0.01570442 |
| No stimulus | 0/3 | 0 | 4.31 × 10⁻¹² | Roundoff-scale | Roundoff-scale |

All three default hosts lose activation when coupling is removed. The blocked recording still contains volume-conducted graft activity and a small host contribution from subthreshold field-induced membrane currents. Thus a receiving-region waveform, or even a nonzero host-source component, does not establish a host action potential. Peak-to-peak component amplitudes are not additive because their extrema can occur at different times.

Instantaneous graft plus host neural signals reconstruct the total neural recording with a maximum residual of **1.3323 × 10⁻¹⁴ µV** across **2,403 contact-time samples**. This partition uses known model source identities; it is not an inverse method that recovers cell origin from an experimental electrode recording. Stimulation artifact remains separately identified: the default R2 ideal artifact is approximately **3,343.99 µV p–p**, much larger than its neural signal. Neither that ideal artifact nor the source-separated waveform models amplifier saturation, electrode impedance, or acquisition recovery.

Two deliberately different controls test the classification:

- A mixed-placement case with `axonLengthUm:300`, `stimXUm:300`, `stimAmplitudeUa:-30`, one pulse, and 25 ms duration recruits all three hosts intact. Hosts 1 and 3 lose activation with block; host 2 persists at 6.1746534 ms. The workbench preserves the per-host outcome instead of relying only on aggregate counts.
- A direct-activation positive control uses one graft, one host, a 300 µm tract, stimulus x = 300 µm and height = 70 µm, −40 µA, one pulse, and 25 ms duration. Its host fires at **6.17150065 ms** with and without coupling. This is correctly classified as activation that persists with block. The matched no-stimulus control is quiet at the monitored sites.

“Quiet” here means no detected upward 0 mV crossings at the monitored soma and terminal locations. It does not imply every compartment is unpolarized.

## Sampled response studies and timing interpretation

All five default five-point grids completed using full default geometry, 40 ms duration, and two pulses. The stored grids are unpaired runs; the workbench additionally supports paired controls. Recruitment is a deterministic count of hosts that spike at least once.

| Varied quantity | Ordered sampled values | Recruited hosts in that order |
|---|---|---|
| Signed stimulus current / µA | −6, −12, −18, −24, −30 | 0, 0, 3, 3, 3 |
| Synaptic weight / µS | 0, 0.005, 0.01, 0.025, 0.05 | 0, 3, 3, 3, 3 |
| Recording height / µm | 60, 100, 150, 200, 300 | 3, 3, 3, 3, 3 |
| Shared axonal diameter / µm | 0.8, 1.2, 2, 3, 4 | 3, 3, 3, 3, 3 |
| Conductivity / S m⁻¹ | 0.15, 0.225, 0.3, 0.45, 0.6 | 3, 3, 3, 3, 0 |

The current result brackets a sampled count change between −12 and −18 µA; it is not an exact threshold. A “nondecreasing” count in this listed sequence does not mean increasing signed current. At recording heights 60 and 300 µm, R2 neural amplitude falls from **14.9936 to 1.78659 µV p–p** while recruitment stays constant. Conductivity affects both the stimulation field and readout in this sweep, so its results must not be interpreted as the fixed-source `1/σ` scaling test.

The diameter parameter is shared by graft trunks and host axons; graft terminal branches use 0.8 times that value. Its sweep cannot attribute every recruitment or recording change solely to the engineered tract. This scope is now explicit in the questions, architecture, controls, and Methods.

A dedicated refinement repeats the fixed −12 and −18 µA endpoints at **0.005 and 0.0025 ms**, retaining the two-pulse 40 ms protocol. At −12 µA, both runs recruit 0/3 hosts. At −18 µA, both recruit 3/3, with **one soma spike per host** at both steps. Fine-minus-coarse shifts for hosts 1–3 are −0.01727043, −0.01974585, and −0.01972369 ms. These differences are reported without adding a post-hoc timing tolerance. The check establishes count agreement at those two endpoints, not uniform convergence over intermediate currents or an exact threshold.

Legacy timing fields are retained for data compatibility but described precisely. `conductionDelayMs` is the first graft-1 monitored tract-tip crossing minus the first graft-1 soma crossing; `relayDelayMs` is the earliest host soma crossing minus the first graft-1 soma crossing. They are signed differences between selected first events. In the mixed-placement case they are **−0.98156206 ms** and **−0.71388448 ms**. Distal activation can precede graft-soma activation; these values are not negative physical travel times. Negative differences remain visible, and apparent velocity is unavailable when its denominator is nonpositive. Positive differences also require wavefront and causal matching before a physical conduction interpretation is justified. JSON exports explicitly define the legacy `successfulTransmission` flag and `relayConsistentHosts` count as temporal consistency, not proof of synaptic dependence.

Unsupported contact placement is retained as an error. A recording-height sweep at x offset −100 µm with heights 45, 60, and 150 µm returns success/error/success: the middle contact intersects the modeled membrane or its 3 µm clearance. That row has no fabricated zero result, and no response bracket is drawn across it.

## Numerical verification and retained discrepancies

The regenerated stage 2 quantitative report has **ten passing acceptance checks**: exact preservation of the frozen released model within the executing runtime; default synaptic dependence; zero-stimulus monitored events; mixed cell-specific attribution; the direct-activation positive control; source additivity; analytical storage-precision consistency; completion of five default grids; fixed-endpoint recruitment under time refinement; and unsupported-placement error retention. All **13 recorded source hashes** match the reviewed files. Both the failed stored-Float32 reconstruction diagnostic and the historical cross-runtime equality failure remain explicitly recorded in the same report.

### Stored-voltage reconstruction: original absolute check failed

An independent diagnostic reconstructs membrane current from axial flux using saved **Float32** compartment voltages, then applies the recording lead field. Original computed source recordings use **Float64**. The provisional absolute reconstruction criterion remains **0.001 µV**:

| Population | Maximum reconstruction error / µV | Original absolute diagnostic |
|---|---:|---|
| Graft | 0.000096390026 | Pass |
| Host | **0.001273569150** | **Fail, retained** |

No tolerance was widened. A separate analytical quantization check propagates each stored voltage's half-ULP through `B = L_source A_axial`: the bound is `Σ |B_j| halfULP(V_j) + 10⁻⁸ µV`. All **4,806 population-contact-time values** satisfy this bound; the largest error-to-bound ratio is **0.606757**. At the worst host sample, the bound is 0.00216670 µV. This explains consistency with storage precision without converting the original absolute failure into a pass. Quantitative source exports retain the original Float64 waveforms.

### Independent native implementation

The native NEURON 9.0.1/Python 3.12.14 comparison covers rest, disconnected stimulation, the intact relay at 0.005 ms, and the relay at 0.0025 ms. It uses native `hh`, `Exp2Syn`, voltage `NetCon`, and independently assembled finite-contact recording/source projections. Comparisons use the same physical times without offsets, alignment, or rescaling.

All four cases pass the retained criteria: voltage RMS ≤ 1 mV; spike-time error ≤ 0.1 ms; equal spike counts; extracellular and each source-component maximum error ≤ 0.001 µV. The separate 0.5 mV pointwise voltage diagnostic also passes. Maximum source errors across cases are **1.5201 × 10⁻¹¹ µV for graft** and **1.2684 × 10⁻¹¹ µV for host**. Agreement concerns independent implementations of the specified equations and discretization; it does not calibrate a biological graft or establish mammalian operating thresholds.

The native endpoint observation remains documented: accumulated native time at 0.0025 ms can lie just below 40 ms, omitting the scheduled terminal sample. Reading the already-computed terminal state when within 10⁻⁸ ms supplies that endpoint without an additional step, interpolation, or time shift. Earlier spatial-gate failures, their target-selection correction, and the event-order defect remain in the validation history.

## Browser verification and remaining limits

The local browser successfully exercised the default causal audit, source charts, and invalid sweep handling. A later Worker start failed after a build; the subsequent reload was blocked by the browser URL policy. This interruption is retained as an unresolved local-browser observation. Passing real-Worker tests and compiled asset checks do not prove that the compiled HTTPS browser deployment starts and completes those calculations. That deployment check remains pending below.

The available verification browser disables WebGL. The anatomical 2D fallback is available; **GPU-enabled 3D rendering and its interactions have not been directly verified in this environment**. Final desktop and mobile layout, compiled Worker startup, cancellation/restart, and browser CSV/JSON download checks must be recorded with their actual outcomes. Automated export integrity checks establish file content and replay contracts, not successful browser download delivery.

Biological limitations remain explicit: deterministic recruitment is not activation probability; geometric encounters are not mature functional synapses; growth is a distinct exploratory days-scale model; conductivity and connection perturbations are not calibrated chronic-implant predictions. Nominal stimulus charge and geometric charge density are ideal calculations, without electrode electrochemistry, tissue-damage calibration, or clinical safety interpretation.
