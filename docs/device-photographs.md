# Device photographs

The device catalog uses real photographs, optical/electron micrographs and clearly labeled published source figures. No generated hardware imagery is used. Complete source figures can contain diagrams alongside their hardware images; their captions distinguish those panels. The manifest is `src/data/device-photos.json`. It records attribution, source and asset URLs, license, source hash, crop bounds, original dimensions, changes and version qualifications. Credits and licenses are visible beside each published image.

## Noncommercial scope

The site owner confirmed strictly noncommercial use on 9 October 2026. NeuroRoots and the Stanford microwire–CMOS photographs are CC BY-NC 4.0 and must not be reused commercially without separate permission. This confirmation does not grant permission to republish arbitrary manufacturer images. No cropped CC BY-NC-ND figures are included. ShareAlike image adaptations remain under the specific image license; this does not relicense the surrounding independent site.

The Connexus image comes from Paradromics' official media press kit and retains the requested Paradromics credit. It is not labeled Creative Commons. Archival or research prototypes are explicitly qualified, including Stentrode, DOT, SetPoint and Cochlear Nucleus. A family photograph does not verify another version's geometry or performance.

The later collection includes complete CC BY-NC-ND figures for mesh electronics, Argo and Argus II, the intact Precision comparison photograph, and an intact iEEG study figure as a secondary view. These files are copied byte-for-byte, including every panel and annotation, and use the same intact file for the thumbnail. The individual figures are not cropped or re-encoded. Original-content press images for PtNRGrid and iEEG, and KAIST's licensed MOTE image, retain the credits and image-specific reuse terms published on EurekAlert. Those permissions are recorded as press-image permissions, not Creative Commons grants. All three press images are also preserved intact.

## Coverage and maintenance

The initial release contained 25 photographed device records. The follow-up release contains 31, adding NET, Flex2Chip, Neurotassel, Axonics, INBRAIN and PRIMA. The Stanford microwire and both DOT records now use higher-resolution originals recovered from the published PDFs, without synthetic enhancement. The source extraction page and image index are retained in the manifest.

The third collection adds 16 pictured records: Neuropixels 1.0, two spiral-cuff catalog records, mesh electronics, NeuroGrid, two Argus II records, Precision Layer 7, PtNRGrid, the iEEG microdisplay, Argo, MOTE, MERF, the mosquito-inspired neural probe, the silk intraventricular interface and SIMIT's 256-channel µECoG implant. There are 47 pictured records among 123 published records, with 76 still pending. Four new catalog records were added concurrently, so the catalog denominator is larger than the earlier 119-record audit. The two spiral-cuff records share one reference photograph, as do the two Argus II records; record counts are not counts of unique hardware photographs.

Seven additional views cover Neuropixels 1.0, NeuroGrid, iEEG, MERF, the mosquito-inspired probe and the silk interface. The catalog keeps one lead image. Device pages use a small thumbnail rail to select another image, with separate provenance and an enlargement dialog for each view. Without JavaScript, all views remain visible and the rail's links navigate to them. Non-selected views load lazily. CMYK source panels are rendered from their published PDF in the original display colors, rather than using an incorrectly decoded negative.

On 10 October 2026, the owner supplied three Neuropixels figures and two Stentrode photographs and explicitly requested their publication. The Neuropixels gallery starts with the family architecture overview, then the 1.0 detail and the 1.0/2.0 comparison. The older assembly photograph and tip view were subsequently removed at the owner’s request. The Stentrode gallery starts with the supplied device view, then the close-up with its 10 mm scale; the older Commons reference photograph was subsequently removed at the owner’s request. Each new upload is preserved byte for byte, including its labels, panels and metadata. The first Neuropixels figure's Ultra assembly is identified as family context, and the 2.0 comparison is identified as 2021 alpha hardware.

Owner-supplied files use an explicit `user_supplied` rights status and retain the original source copyrights. This records the owner's publication instruction without asserting a Creative Commons license or a manufacturer republication grant. The source URLs identify the primary Neuropixels studies or provide Synchron device background; they are not claimed as the exact downloaded source assets. The upload filename and hash identify the actual bytes. Other retained, separately licensed views keep their original attribution and license. Other pending candidates are unaffected.

The Neuralink N1 page now includes two explicitly dated historical references from the 2019 research white paper: complete Figure 1 (1762 × 1240) and complete Figure 6 (5252 × 1835). Both the catalog caption and device gallery identify this as older research hardware, not the current N1 implant. Figure 6 retains both the cortical implantation panel and the rat wearing its wired recording package. The native embedded images were recovered from the public author white-paper PDF, with transparency composited on the original white page background. Every panel, scale and annotation is retained under CC BY-ND 4.0; the recovered PNGs are copied intact, with no cropped thumbnail. The source PDF URL, primary publication, attribution, license and extraction details are recorded per view. This does not clear reuse of current N1 manufacturer images.

The follow-up source review is tracked for every published device record in `src/data/device-photo-research.json`. Pending candidates are not public gallery images. Some need copyright permission, a clearer implanted-part view, a verified hardware version or photographic-type verification. Absence of an image does not mean no photograph exists. Two DOT records reference the same published prototype; they are not distinct hardware photographs. Altius remains pending: its article clears noncommercial reuse but does not establish that its polished product image is a camera photograph. The MIT fiber press image is licensed but is a researcher portrait, not a useful implanted-part close-up.

Use `scripts/prepare-device-photo.mjs` for reproducible panel extraction and WebP optimization. Use `--view=slug` for another view and `--preserve-source` to copy an intact image without any transformation. Do not remove original scale bars, fabricate dimensions, recolor hardware or replace real devices with generated images. Keep each source hash and extraction rectangle in the manifest and verify the local image dimensions. Recheck licensing before any commercial change to the site.

Tests cover every view's catalog ID, source/license fields, actual file dimensions and sizes, preservation hashes, family/version attribution and viewer accessibility structure. Browser interaction and visual layout require a separate browser check when browser QA is available; that capability was unavailable for this collection. Asset crops were visually inspected and the production build checked on Node 24 and Node 22.16.0. A release is only reported live after deployment succeeds and deployed files match their local hashes.
