# Allen Institute

The Allen Institute is a private, nonprofit (501(c)(3)) bioscience research institute in Seattle, founded in 2003 by Microsoft co-founder Paul G. Allen and his sister Jody Allen, who serves as Board Chair today. It runs large-scale "big science, team science, open science" projects and releases all data and resources publicly. The Institute states it is supported primarily by the Fund for Science and Technology. Wikipedia lists roughly 825 employees; the Institute's own pages do not state a current headcount.

Paul Allen's 2003 seed commitment of $100M created the Allen Institute for Brain Science and its first project, the Allen Brain Atlas. In 2012 he pledged a further $300M, bringing his total commitment to $500M. After his death in October 2018, a $125M gift announced that December launched the Allen Institute for Immunology.

A 2020 reorganization moved Brain Science onto a 16-year cell-types and connectivity phase and spun the MindScope program (Christof Koch, Chief Scientist) and the Allen Brain Observatory out of Brain Science into a separate program. The Institute's current site describes its research through "accelerators" covering brain science, cell science, neural dynamics, immunology, synthetic biology, AI biodesign and brain health.

## Allen Brain Atlas family

All atlas resources are free through the [brain-map.org](https://brain-map.org/atlases) portal.

| Year | Resource | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2006 | [Allen Mouse Brain Atlas](https://mouse.brain-map.org/) | Genome-wide 3D map of gene expression in the adult mouse brain; over 21,000 genes at cellular resolution. Completed September 26, 2006 as the Institute's inaugural project. |
| 2008 | Allen Spinal Cord Atlas | Genome-wide gene expression across the mouse spinal cord (launched July 17, 2008). |
| 2008 | Allen Developing Mouse Brain Atlas | Gene activity across embryonic and postnatal developmental time points (launched November 14, 2008). |
| 2010 | [Allen Human Brain Atlas](https://human.brain-map.org/) | MRI, diffusion tensor, histology and microarray/ISH gene expression in adult human brain (launched May 24, 2010; profiled in Nature 2012). |
| 2011 | Allen Mouse Brain Connectivity Atlas | 3D high-resolution map of neural connections across the mouse brain (launched November 3, 2011). |
| 2015 | [Allen Cell Types Database](https://celltypes.brain-map.org/) | Morphological, electrophysiological and transcriptomic cell type data; first release covered 240+ mouse cells, human cells added in 2017. |
| 2016 | Allen Brain Observatory | Standardized platform recording cellular-level activity in mouse visual cortex (two-photon calcium, later Neuropixels); more than 65,000 neurons deposited by 2018. |
| 2018 | OpenScope | Opened the Brain Observatory to experiments proposed by external scientists, modeled on shared astronomy observatories (launched July 26, 2018). |
| 2023 | [Allen Brain Cell (ABC) Atlas](https://portal.brain-biccn.org/) | Whole adult mouse brain cell-type atlas with BICCN: ~7M scRNA-seq cells profiled (~4.0M after QC) plus ~4.3M MERFISH cells, organized as 34 classes, 338 subclasses, 1,201 supertypes and 5,322 clusters on the Allen CCFv3 reference (Zhang et al., Nature 2023). |

## Neuropixels and electrophysiology

Per the [Neuropixels about page](https://www.neuropixels.org/about), the probes were developed through an international collaboration funded by HHMI, the Wellcome Trust, the Gatsby Charitable Foundation and the Allen Institute for Brain Science, and were designed, developed and fabricated at imec with Janelia Research Campus, the Allen Institute for Brain Science and UCL. Cataloged hardware on this site: [Neuropixels 1.0](/devices/04-neuropixels-probe/), [Neuropixels Ultra](/devices/83-neuropixels-ultra-high-density-probe/), [Neuropixels 1.0 NHP long-shank](/devices/85-neuropixels-10-nhp-long-shank/), [Neuropixels 2.0 alpha](/devices/88-neuropixels-20-alpha-probe/), [Neuropixels 2.0 Quad Base](/devices/104-neuropixels-20-quad-base-probe/).

The Allen Brain Observatory's [Visual Coding Neuropixels dataset](https://allensdk.readthedocs.io/en/latest/visual_coding_neuropixels.html) records with up to six Neuropixels probes across mouse visual cortex and thalamus during passive visual stimulation; spike-sorted data ship as NWB files through the AllenSDK with per-unit quality metrics. The analysis paper is Siegle et al. 2021, "Survey of spiking in the mouse visual system reveals functional hierarchy" (Nature).

## Allen Institute for Cell Science

Founded in 2014 with a contribution from Paul G. Allen (press reports put it at $100M). Its [Allen Cell Explorer](https://www.allencell.org/about.html) portal publishes gene-edited, fluorescently tagged human iPS cell lines (available to outside labs), large-scale 3D microscopy (the 3D Cell Viewer lists 31,987 images), and deep-learning models of cell organization (Allen Integrated Cell).

## Open datasets and tools

- [brain-map.org atlases portal](https://brain-map.org/atlases): mouse, human, spinal cord, developing brain and connectivity atlases.
- [Allen Cell Types Database](https://celltypes.brain-map.org/).
- [Allen Brain Observatory](https://observatory.brain-map.org/) and OpenScope data.
- [AllenSDK](https://allensdk.readthedocs.io/): Python API and notebooks for the atlases, Cell Types and observatory data.
- [ABC Atlas / BICCN portal](https://portal.brain-biccn.org/): the Institute hosts the BRAIN Initiative Cell Census Network data portal.
- [Allen Cell Explorer](https://www.allencell.org/): Cell Science data, cell catalog, viewers and models.
- MICrONS: the Allen Institute is a partner in the IARPA-funded program that mapped the synapses of one cubic millimeter of mouse visual cortex, described at launch as the largest connectomics project to date.

A 2020 NIH award of $40.5M established an Alzheimer's disease brain-cell-types research center headquartered at the Institute.

## Neuropixels Opto prototype collaboration

This entry also carries the Institute's role in the 2026 Neuropixels Opto collaboration. The 2026 paper lists affiliations at Allen Institute for Neural Dynamics, Allen Institute for Brain Science and Allen Institute MindScope in Seattle, with Joshua Siegle (Allen) and Matteo Carandini (UCL) named as joint supervisors. Anna Lakunina, Karolina Socha and Alexander Ladd are equal-contribution authors. These roles are directly stated, not inferred from author order. The paper also lists the University of Washington, Janelia Research Campus and Johns Hopkins; see the [Johns Hopkins University, Harris lab](/companies/42-jhu-harris-high-capacity-electrophysiology-lab/) brief. Separate entries cover [University College London, Carandini lab](/companies/50-ucl-carandini-lab/) and [IMEC](/companies/51-imec/).

The UCL program page names funding from Wellcome, Allen, HHMI, NTNU and NIH BRAIN among other sources. No amount, price, licence or corporate ownership is inferred.

### Prototype status

The [2026 Neuropixels Opto prototype](/devices/107-neuropixels-opto-photonic-prototype/) is a prototype, not a product release. UCL expects availability to the community in 2028; that is a forecast, not current availability or a price. Applications: [paper overview](/applications/108-neuropixels-opto-mouse-optotagging-2026/), [cortical activation and inhibition](/applications/141-neuropixels-opto-cortical-activation-inhibition/), [parallel optotagging](/applications/142-neuropixels-opto-parallel-optotagging/).
