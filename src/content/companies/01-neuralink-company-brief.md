## The platform

Neuralink is building an *implantable, wireless intracortical recording system* and a *surgical robot* intended to place fine electrode threads into cortex. Its stated early medical focus is helping people with severe paralysis control digital devices. <sup><a href="#source-01" aria-label="Source 01: Neuralink official website">01</a></sup>

The system can be understood as three connected layers:

1. *The implant.* Intracortical hardware records neural activity and transmits data wirelessly to external devices.

2. *The surgical robot.* A robotic system is designed to insert fine electrode threads with precision.

3. *The decoder.* Software maps recorded neural signals to cursor movement and other device actions.

> Record activity in motor-related cortex, decode intent, then translate that intent into device control.

The strategy pairs high-channel-count recording with surgical automation and an ambition for wireless, at-home usability. Those goals sit within a demanding medical and regulatory pathway.

For the implant’s geometry, specifications and source figures, see the [Neuralink N1 device brief](/devices/02-neuralink-n1/).

## Reported milestones

The dates matter: participant counts and production plans describe particular reports, rather than a live tally of the program.

### January 2024 · first human implant

Early reporting described the first human implant. A Fox Business report attributed the announcement and early neuron-spike detection to Musk. <sup><a href="#source-04" aria-label="Source 04: First human implant report">04</a></sup>

### June 2025 · clinical trials and funding

Reuters reported trials across multiple countries and *five participants with severe paralysis* using the system to control digital and physical devices. The same report described a *$650 million* funding round and reported breakthrough-device tags for speech and vision projects. <sup><a href="#source-05" aria-label="Source 05: Reuters June 2025 report">05</a></sup>

### January 2026 · scale and participant reports

Reuters reported Musk’s stated goals for high-volume device production and a fully automated surgical procedure in 2026. These are *reported goals*, not confirmation that the manufacturing or surgical milestones were reached. <sup><a href="#source-06" aria-label="Source 06: Reuters production and automation report">06</a></sup>

A January 2026 secondary report described approximately *21 trial participants* across several countries. That is a later, dated report than the five-person figure from June 2025; consult Neuralink’s own updates for subsequent developments. <sup><a href="#source-07" aria-label="Source 07: January 2026 participant report">07</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-08" aria-label="Source 08: Neuralink company updates">08</a></sup>

## Questions to watch

1. *Long-term reliability.* Signal stability over months and years, encapsulation, thread durability and an explant or replacement strategy all affect usefulness.

2. *Surgical throughput and risk.* The question is whether automation reduces procedure time, cost and complications at scale.

3. *Usable information throughput.* Channel count does not directly measure degrees of freedom. Stable decoding performance over time matters more than an isolated hardware number.

4. *The clinical pathway.* Paralysis and cursor control are the stated early focus. Broader speech, vision and neurological applications require evidence of their own.
