Neuralink is one of the highest-profile brain–computer interface companies. It is building an *implantable, wireless intracortical recording system* and a *surgical robot* intended to place fine electrode threads into cortex. The company’s early medical focus is helping people with severe paralysis control digital devices. <sup><a href="#source-01" aria-label="Source 01: Neuralink">01</a></sup>

## The platform

Neuralink’s system has three major pieces:

1. *Implant.* A brain implant records neural activity and transmits data wirelessly to external devices.
2. *Surgical robot.* A robotic system inserts electrode threads with high precision.
3. *Decoding/software layer.* Software maps recorded neural signals to a cursor and other device actions.

In plain terms: *record spikes in motor-related cortex → decode intent → move a cursor*.

For implant geometry, specifications, images and the 3D model, see the [Neuralink N1 device brief](/devices/02-neuralink-n1/).

### More stable decoding

On October 1, 2026, Neuralink described self-supervised encoders trained on *more than 50,000 hours* of neural recordings. It reported decoders working for weeks, reduced calibration for some users, and a cursor-control result of *11.32 bits per second*. These results address a central challenge: keeping the interface useful as neural signals drift. <sup><a href="#source-10" aria-label="Source 10: Neuralink decoder update">10</a></sup>

## Clinical progress

### First human implant

The first human implantation took place in January 2024, beginning the company’s human testing program. Early reporting described detection of neuron spikes. <sup><a href="#source-04" aria-label="Source 04: First human implant report">04</a></sup>

### Participants and trials

Neura Pod’s October 2026 patient table lists *27+ publicly documented implant recipients*. Neuralink’s January 2026 company update had announced 21 participants. <sup><a href="#source-11" aria-label="Source 11: October 2026 public participant count">11</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-07" aria-label="Source 07: Neuralink January 2026 participant update">07</a></sup>

*PRIME* investigates computer and robotic-arm control; *VOICE* studies speech restoration. The N1 remains an investigational device. <sup><a href="#source-08" aria-label="Source 08: Neuralink clinical trials">08</a></sup>

### Surgery and production

Neuralink’s 2026 goals include higher-volume production and greater surgical automation. Reuters reported these plans in January; they describe the intended scaling path. <sup><a href="#source-06" aria-label="Source 06: Production and surgical automation goals">06</a></sup>

## Company and funding

Neuralink now lists its headquarters in *Austin, Texas*, with operations also in Fremont and South San Francisco. ## The BCI landscape

Neuralink pairs *high-channel-count intracortical recording* with a *hardware and surgical-automation strategy*. Wireless, at-home use is central to its usability goals.

## Key questions

1. *Long-term reliability.* Signal stability over months and years; electrode encapsulation; durability of threads; explant and replacement strategy.
2. *Surgical throughput and risk.* Whether automation reduces time, cost and complication rates at scale.
3. *True information throughput.* Channel count is not the same as usable degrees of freedom. The key is stable decoding performance over time.
4. *Clinical expansion.* Paralysis and cursor control are the first focus. Speech, vision and other neurological applications require their own clinical evidence.
