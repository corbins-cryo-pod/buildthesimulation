The *Viventi Lab* at Duke University develops flexible electronics to interface with the brain. Its research page says micro-electrocorticography gives a high-resolution interface with the cortex and that the lab aims to build devices that are stable, repeatable and easy to build, to use in house and share with other researchers.

- Lab site: <https://viventi.pratt.duke.edu/>
- Research overview: <https://viventi.pratt.duke.edu/research>

### Related work listed on the lab page

- Chiang et al., *Science Translational Medicine* 12(538), 2020, a neural interface for high-definition, long-term recording in rodents and nonhuman primates, with John A. Rogers among the authors.

### Limits

The 2011 device paper's senior authors include John A. Rogers and Brian Litt; this brief does not say where each author worked in 2011.
