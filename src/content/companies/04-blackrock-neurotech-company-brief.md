Blackrock Neurotech is one of the longest-running infrastructure companies in invasive BCI. It supplies *implantable electrode arrays* and a *recording ecosystem* used across academic human BCI research. Its core legacy technology is the *Utah Array*, also called the *NeuroPort Electrode*, alongside headstages, amplifiers and software. <sup><a href="#source-03" aria-label="Source 03: Blackrock technology ecosystem">03</a></sup>

## The technology

Blackrock builds a full ecosystem:

- Implantable electrodes: Utah Array and NeuroPort Electrode
- Headstages and front-end acquisition
- Neural signal processing hardware
- Software and research tools

### Utah Array

The Utah Array is a rigid, 3D microelectrode array used for *single-unit and multiunit recordings* from cortex. Its classical configuration has roughly 100 electrodes. <sup><a href="#source-04" aria-label="Source 04: Utah Array background">04</a></sup>

Blackrock’s technology has been implanted in humans since 2004. In an August 2026 interview, CEO Marcus Gerhardt described *56 implant recipients* and *55,000 patient-days* of brain data. <sup><a href="#source-03" aria-label="Source 03: Blackrock human research history">03</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-10" aria-label="Source 10: Marcus Gerhardt August 2026 interview">10</a></sup>

Explore implant geometry, specifications, images and the 3D model in the [Utah Microelectrode Array device brief](/devices/01-utah-microelectrode-array/).

### MoveAgain

*MoveAgain* is Blackrock’s bridge from research systems toward clinical use. The company reports that it received FDA Breakthrough Device Designation in 2021. <sup><a href="#source-01" aria-label="Source 01: Blackrock Neurotech">01</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-03" aria-label="Source 03: MoveAgain designation and technology">03</a></sup>

## Research demonstrations

Human BCI research is often driven by consortia such as BrainGate and UCSF. These studies show what cortical interfaces can enable across different electrode technologies.

### Speech decoding

Moses and colleagues demonstrated decoding attempted speech into words and sentences in a person with anarthria using cortical recordings, deep learning and language-model decoding. The 2021 *New England Journal of Medicine* study used a *cortical-surface ECoG array*; it illustrates the wider BCI field rather than Utah-array performance. <sup><a href="#source-05" aria-label="Source 05: Moses et al. speech decoding study">05</a></sup>

### Wireless recording at home

Brown University reported human use of a high-bandwidth wireless intracortical BCI at home in 2021. Its report describes a licensing agreement with Blackrock Microsystems to make the device available to researchers. <sup><a href="#source-06" aria-label="Source 06: Brown University wireless BCI report">06</a></sup>

### Evoked visual percepts

Fernández and colleagues demonstrated visual percepts using a *96-channel intracortical array* in human occipital cortex. The 2021 *Journal of Clinical Investigation* paper connects intracortical stimulation with artificial vision. <sup><a href="#source-07" aria-label="Source 07: Fernández et al. visual percept study">07</a></sup>

## Key questions

1. *Longevity and signal stability.* Chronic unit yield, tissue encapsulation and connector reliability remain central engineering constraints.
2. *Surgical burden and infection risk.* Many historical deployments used percutaneous connectors. Fully implanted clinical systems need telemetry and power solutions.
3. *Scaling beyond 100 channels.* Richer control often requires multiple arrays or different electrode technologies.
4. *Clinical commercialization.* Turning research performance into a product involves training burden, calibration drift, long-term support and regulatory requirements.
