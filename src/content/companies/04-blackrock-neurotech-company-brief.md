## The platform

Blackrock Neurotech is one of the longest-running infrastructure companies in invasive BCI. Its implantable electrode arrays and recording ecosystem have supported a substantial body of academic human BCI research. The core legacy platform combines the *Utah Array*, also called the *NeuroPort Electrode*, with headstages, amplifiers and software. <sup><a href="#source-03" aria-label="Source 03: Blackrock technology ecosystem">03</a></sup>

The company describes an ecosystem that spans implantable electrodes, front-end acquisition, neural signal processing and software. This brief concentrates on that technical stack and the public evidence behind it.

### Utah Array

The Utah Array is a rigid, three-dimensional microelectrode array used for *single-unit and multiunit cortical recordings*. Its familiar geometry has become a reference point for intracortical interfaces. <sup><a href="#source-04" aria-label="Source 04: Utah Array background">04</a></sup>

Blackrock states that its technology has been implanted in humans since 2004, and makes a broad claim about its prevalence in human BCI work. Those statements are *company claims*, rather than an independently audited market share. <sup><a href="#source-03" aria-label="Source 03: Blackrock technology claims">03</a></sup>

Explore the geometry and hardware in the [Utah Microelectrode Array device brief](/devices/01-utah-microelectrode-array/).

### MoveAgain

Blackrock presents *MoveAgain* as its first device designed for clinical use and reports an FDA Breakthrough Device Designation. The designation claim is attributed to the company; it has not been independently checked here against an FDA listing. <sup><a href="#source-01" aria-label="Source 01: Blackrock official website">01</a></sup> <sup><a href="#source-03" aria-label="Source 03: MoveAgain company description">03</a></sup>

## Research in context

Human BCI demonstrations often come from academic consortia such as BrainGate and UCSF. The studies below help explain what cortical interfaces can enable; their results should not all be attributed to Blackrock or to a single electrode technology.

### Wireless recording at home

Brown University reported human use of a high-bandwidth wireless intracortical BCI in 2021, including use at home. Its report describes a licensing agreement with Blackrock Microsystems to make the device available to researchers. <sup><a href="#source-06" aria-label="Source 06: Brown University wireless BCI report">06</a></sup>

### Evoked visual percepts

Fernández and colleagues studied visual percepts evoked by a *96-channel intracortical array* implanted in human occipital cortex. Published in the *Journal of Clinical Investigation* in 2021, the study anchors the discussion of intracortical stimulation in a human demonstration. <sup><a href="#source-07" aria-label="Source 07: Fernández et al. visual percept study">07</a></sup>

### Speech decoding · wider BCI context

Moses and colleagues demonstrated attempted-speech decoding in a person with anarthria using cortical recordings, deep learning and language-model decoding. This 2021 *New England Journal of Medicine* study used a *cortical-surface ECoG array*. It belongs to the wider BCI context, rather than serving as evidence for Utah-array performance. <sup><a href="#source-05" aria-label="Source 05: Moses et al. speech decoding study">05</a></sup>

## Questions to watch

1. *Longevity and signal stability.* Chronic unit yield, tissue encapsulation and connector reliability remain central engineering constraints, even with the Utah Array’s long research history.

2. *Surgical burden and infection risk.* Many historical deployments used percutaneous connectors. Fully implanted systems bring their own telemetry and power requirements.

3. *Scaling beyond an array.* A classical Utah Array has roughly 100 electrodes. Richer control can require multiple arrays or alternative electrode technologies.

4. *Clinical commercialization.* Turning research performance into a supported product involves training, calibration drift, long-term service and regulatory requirements.
