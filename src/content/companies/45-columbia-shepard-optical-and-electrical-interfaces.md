# Columbia University, Shepard lab

The 2024 SCOPe paper lists Kenneth Shepard with Columbia electrical engineering, biomedical engineering and neurological surgery. This entry covers the [SCOPe optical probe](/devices/137-scope-subdural-cmos-optical-probe/) and the [BISC electrical surface array](/devices/73-bisc-wireless-subdural-cmos-array/), which are not interchangeable implants. SCOPe applications cover [mouse optical tests](/applications/138-scope-mouse-bidirectional-optical-tests/) and [macaque speed decoding](/applications/139-scope-macaque-reach-speed-decoding/).

## Collaborators named in the paper

The paper lists optical-interface collaborators at NYU, University of Pennsylvania, Rice, University of Washington and the John B. Pierce Laboratory/Yale. Bijan Pesaran is listed with NYU and Penn departments. Jacob Robinson and Ashok Veeraraghavan are listed with Rice electrical engineering; see the [Rice University, Robinson lab](/companies/43-rice-magnetoelectric-bioelectronics-labs/). Vincent Pieribone is listed with John B. Pierce Laboratory and Yale. These are reported affiliations, not assignments of each circuit or animal study.

## Two distinct interface families

SCOPe uses SPAD imaging, microLED excitation/stimulation and computational reconstruction of genetically encoded calcium signals. Its demonstrated controller is wired. BISC records electrical surface signals and has separately documented wireless power and telemetry. SCOPe's optical evidence does not extend BISC's channel count, and BISC's wireless link does not make SCOPe wireless.

The published supplement is accessible; animal details from the 2023 preprint remain labeled as that version. No commercialization claim, grant, licence, clinical approval or map coordinate is added.
