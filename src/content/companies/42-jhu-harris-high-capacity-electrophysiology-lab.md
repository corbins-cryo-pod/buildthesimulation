# Johns Hopkins Harris lab

The current lab website places the Harris laboratory in Johns Hopkins University's Department of Biomedical Engineering. The university faculty profile identifies Tim Harris as a research professor who shares his time with the HHMI Janelia Research Campus. The lab describes engineering and neuroscience work on high-capacity electrophysiology in behaving animals.

This is separate from the [Johns Hopkins APL prosthetic-limb group](/companies/39-jhu-apl-modular-prosthetic-limb-lab-brief/). A common university name does not assign Neuropixels work to APL or prosthetic-limb results to this laboratory.

## Cataloged work

- [Neuropixels 2.0 Quad Base hardware](/devices/104-neuropixels-20-quad-base-probe/) and its [July 2026 mouse sensorimotor preprint](/applications/105-neuropixels-quad-base-mouse-sequences-2026/). The primary affiliations include Johns Hopkins neuroscience and biomedical engineering, Janelia and imec. This link does not make one laboratory the sole owner of the collaboration.
- [NXT / planned NP3.0 prototype](/devices/106-neuropixels-nxt-np30-prototype/). The official project source identifies Harris as PI and Dan O'Connor as co-investigator. Prototype access and 2027 purchase availability remain plans.

## Claims and limits

The faculty profile describes capacities of 10,000-20,000 neurons in rodents and 30,000-50,000 in non-human primates. These are broad institutional platform statements, not a participant-level dataset in the fetched page. They are not used as the yield of the six-mouse Quad Base preprint or as proof of chronic tracking across those neurons.

This brief does not assert human clinical qualification, complete brain coverage, therapeutic outcomes or a stimulation protocol. Precise map coordinates are omitted; the department's address does not establish a specific laboratory location.
