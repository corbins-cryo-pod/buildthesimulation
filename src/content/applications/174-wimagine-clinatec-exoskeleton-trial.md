# WIMAGINE epidural BCI exoskeleton trial (NCT02550522)

The ongoing Clinatec trial of the [WIMAGINE wireless epidural ECoG implant](/devices/07-wimagine-cea-clinatec-epidural-wireless-ecog/) for motor restoration in tetraplegia, best known for the 24-month proof-of-concept in which one participant drove a virtual avatar and a four-limb exoskeleton from decoded epidural signals. Registry facts are from ClinicalTrials.gov (verified October 28, 2026); results are from the peer-reviewed Lancet Neurology report and a follow-up stability study.

## Study facts

| Field | Value and source scope |
| --- | --- |
| Registry | NCT02550522; interventional, single-group, treatment purpose, no masking [1] |
| Official title | Brain Computer Interface: Neuroprosthetic Control of a Motorized Exoskeleton [1] |
| Sponsor and site | University Hospital, Grenoble; CLINATEC, Grenoble, France [1] |
| Status | Recruiting [1] |
| Dates | Started September 2015; primary completion and study completion estimated April 2033 [1] |
| Enrollment | 5 (estimated) [1] |
| Population | Traumatic tetraplegia with cervical cord injury [1] |
| Intervention | BCI platform with two implanted, remotely powered epidural ECoG recording devices driving an exoskeleton [1] |
| Primary outcome | Number of participants with adverse events, 4 years after surgery [1] |
| Secondary outcomes | Performance in piloting the degrees of freedom of the exoskeleton; quality-of-life evaluation (both 4 years after surgery) [1] |

## Published results

| Field | Value and source scope |
| --- | --- |
| Cohort | Two participants recruited into the ongoing trial; one excluded after a technical problem with the implants; the remaining participant was a 28-year-old man with C4-C5 tetraplegia [2] |
| Implant | Two bilateral wireless epidural recorders, 64 electrodes each, over the upper-limb sensorimotor areas [2] |
| Reporting window | June 12, 2017 to July 21, 2019; 24 months of progressive training [2] |
| Performance | Cortical control of a walking simulation and bimanual, multi-joint upper-limb movements with eight degrees of freedom: 64.0% (SD 5.1) success with a virtual avatar at home, 70.9% (SD 11.6) with the exoskeleton in the laboratory [2] |
| Decoder stability | Decoding models reusable for up to about 7 weeks without recalibration [2] |
| Recorder stability | A 2021 follow-up characterized the long-term stability of the chronic WIMAGINE epidural recorders in tetraplegic patients [3] |
