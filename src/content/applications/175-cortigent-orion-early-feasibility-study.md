# Cortigent Orion early feasibility study (NCT03344848)

The early feasibility study of the [Orion visual cortical prosthesis](/devices/166-cortigent-orion-visual-cortical-prosthesis/), which bypasses the eyes and optic nerve to stimulate the visual cortex directly in blind adults. Registry facts are from ClinicalTrials.gov (verified October 28, 2026); interim results are from an ophthalmology-conference report of the sponsor's presentation, and the phosphene-mapping studies in trial participants are peer reviewed.

## Study facts

| Field | Value and source scope |
| --- | --- |
| Registry | NCT03344848; interventional, single-group, device feasibility, no masking [1] |
| Official title | Early Feasibility Study of the Orion Visual Cortical Prosthesis System [1] |
| Sponsor | Second Sight Medical Products (the Orion program later moved to Cortigent) [1] |
| Sites | University of California, Los Angeles; Baylor College of Medicine, Houston [1] |
| Status | Active, not recruiting [1] |
| Dates | Actual start November 20, 2017; primary completion and study completion estimated March 31, 2025 [1] |
| Enrollment | 6 (estimated) [1] |
| Population | Acquired blindness [1] |
| Intervention | Orion Visual Cortical Prosthesis System: a 60-electrode array on the visual cortex, driven by a camera in the patient's glasses [1][2] |
| Primary outcome | Rate of device- or procedure-related adverse events, through study completion (average 6 years) [1] |
| Secondary outcome | Ability to create visual perception, measured by stimulation thresholds at 1, 6, 12, 24, 60 and 72 months [1] |

## Reported results

| Field | Value and source scope |
| --- | --- |
| Interim safety (12 months) | Five nonserious adverse events and one serious adverse event (a seizure); four of six patients had no device- or procedure-related adverse event; safety profile described as favorable [2] |
| Phosphene perception | All six subjects perceived phosphenes at 12 months after implantation; the vast majority of the 60 contacts over primary and secondary visual cortices produced a perceived phosphene [2] |
| Functional report | On observer-rated functional low-vision assessments, five patients experienced positive improvements and one patient mild positive improvements at 12 months; the investigator also described Orion performance as at least equivalent to the Argus II retinal implant at 12 months (investigator-reported) [2] |
| Phosphene mapping | In blind Orion participants, multi-electrode stimulation evoked consistent spatial phosphene patterns; bimanual tactile fixation and relative multi-point mapping improved localization consistency [3] |
| Report status | Interim results presented at the American Academy of Ophthalmology 2019 annual meeting and covered in trade press; no peer-reviewed primary outcomes paper identified as of October 28, 2026 [2] |
