# Intracortical auditory-feedback speller in completely locked-in ALS, 2022

A spelling interface for a man with ALS in the completely locked-in state (CLIS), who could not use an eye tracker because he lacked reliable voluntary eye-movement control. The patient learned to modulate firing rates in response to auditory feedback tones, then used the same yes/no approach to select letters. Authors include Ujwal Chaudhary, Ioannis Vlachos and Jonas Zimmermann (equal first authors) and, as last author, Niels Birbaumer. Published in Nature Communications, March 2022.

## Participants and hardware

- One participant. The paper says the procedure was approved by the German BfArM and the study was declared a single case study under a special authorization.
- Two microelectrode arrays (8 by 8 electrodes each, 1.5 mm length, 0.4 mm pitch; Blackrock Microsystems) implanted in March 2019 in the dominant left motor cortex, with a percutaneous pedestal connector. The catalog's [Utah microelectrode array sheet](/devices/01-utah-microelectrode-array/) covers the array family.
- Affiliations in the paper's record include the University of Tübingen, the Wyss Center for Bio and Neuroengineering in Geneva, München Klinik Bogenhausen, Brown University and others.

## Results as stated

| Measure | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Neurofeedback | 4936 of 5700 trials correct (86.6%) in the 281 blocks before the speller |
| Speller days | Attempted on 107 of 135 days; not attempted on 28 days because the neurofeedback criterion was not reached |
| Intelligible output | 44 of the 107 speller days, rated by three observers |
| Rate | 5747 characters over 5338 minutes, an average of 1.08 characters per minute (median 1.1 per block; range 0.2 to 5.1) |
| Session length | On average 121 minutes of spelling and 131 characters per day |

## Limits

The authors state that communication rates were much lower than in other intracortical studies of people who are not completely locked in. This is a single case. The authors say neurons were recorded on many channels, so loss of recordable waveforms does not explain the lower performance.
