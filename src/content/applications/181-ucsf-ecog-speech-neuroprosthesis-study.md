# UCSF ECoG speech neuroprosthesis study (NCT03698149)

The registered study behind the [PMT high-density subdural ECoG array with percutaneous connector](/devices/63-pmt-high-density-ecog-array-percutaneous-connector/): a single-arm device-feasibility study at UCSF testing ECoG signals for communication and neuroprosthetic control in adults with severe neurological disability. Registry facts are from ClinicalTrials.gov (verified October 31, 2026). Unlike most registered BCI studies in this catalog, this one has peer-reviewed landmark results, listed below.

## Study facts (NCT03698149)

| Field | Value and source scope |
| --- | --- |
| Registry | NCT03698149; "A High-Performance ECoG-based Neural Interface for Communication and Neuroprosthetic Control"; interventional, single-group, no masking, device feasibility [1] |
| Sponsor | Karunesh Ganguly (UCSF), with the National Institute on Deafness and Other Communication Disorders (NIDCD) as collaborator [1] |
| Status and dates | Recruiting; actual start November 9, 2018; estimated primary completion and completion August 15, 2030 [1] |
| Enrollment | 3 (estimated) [1] |
| Site | University of California San Francisco; participants must live within a two-hour drive of UCSF [1] |
| Population | Limited upper-limb use from stroke, ALS, multiple sclerosis, cervical spinal cord injury, brainstem stroke, muscular dystrophy, myopathy or severe neuropathy; Modified Rankin Scale score of 4 or greater with loss of independence; at least 1 year since onset for stroke or spinal cord injury; age over 21 [1] |
| Primary outcomes | Incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events; NIDCD objectives to enable communication via text and via synthesized speech decoded from neural signals, each up to 6 years post-implant [1] |
| Secondary outcomes | Evaluation of communication via decoded text and via decoded synthesized speech, up to 6 years post-implant [1] |

## Published results from this study

| Field | Value and source scope |
| --- | --- |
| 2021 anarthria paper | Neuroprosthesis for decoding speech in a paralyzed person with anarthria (Moses, Metzger, Liu et al., NEJM 2021): full-sentence decoding from attempted speech with a 128-electrode array at 4 mm pitch (peer reviewed) [2] |
| 2023 speech and avatar paper | A high-performance neuroprosthesis for speech decoding and avatar control (Nature, August 2023): text decoding at 78 words per minute median and intelligible synthesized speech with an avatar, with a 253-electrode array at 3 mm pitch (peer reviewed) [3] |
| Version boundary | The published implants were customized array-and-connector combinations, not a single commercial system; the two papers differ in electrode count and spacing, and the registry record does not specify hardware [1][2][3] |
