# Nevro SENZA-RCT pivotal trial (NCT01609972)

The pivotal trial behind Nevro's Senza system: 10 kHz spinal cord stimulation (HF10 therapy), which relieves pain without the paresthesias of traditional low-frequency stimulation, compared head to head against traditional low-frequency SCS in chronic back and leg pain. Registry facts are from ClinicalTrials.gov (verified November 12, 2026); the registry record is stale, so enrollment and status below come from the peer-reviewed reports and the FDA summary. Results are peer reviewed through 24 months; the trial was the basis for FDA PMA P130022 (May 8, 2015), which carried a superiority label over traditional SCS.

## Study facts

| Field | Value and source scope |
| --- | --- |
| Registry | NCT01609972; interventional, randomized, parallel-arm, no masking (open label) [1] |
| Brief title | Comparison of Senza to Commercial Spinal Cord Stimulation for the Treatment of Chronic Pain [1] |
| Sponsor | Nevro Corp [1] |
| Registry status | Unknown (registry record not kept current; estimated completion June 2015). The trial completed and has peer-reviewed results through 24 months [1][2][3] |
| Sites | 10 comprehensive pain treatment centers in the USA per the peer-reviewed reports; 11 sites per the FDA SSED [2][3][5] |
| Enrollment | 198 subjects randomized 1:1 (101 HF10, 97 traditional SCS); 171 passed a temporary trial and were implanted (90 HF10, 81 traditional). The FDA SSED cohort is 241 patients; the registry lists 356 (estimated) [1][2][3][5] |
| Dates | Enrolled June 7 to December 28, 2012 per the FDA SSED; registry start June 2012 [1][5] |
| Population | Chronic back and leg pain; implanted subjects averaged 54.9 years old, 13.6 years since diagnosis, 86.6% had prior back surgery, 88.3% were taking opioid analgesics [3] |
| Primary outcome | Responder: 50% or greater back pain reduction with no stimulation-related neurological deficit; noninferiority then superiority testing [1][2] |

## Results (peer reviewed)

| Field | Value and source scope |
| --- | --- |
| Primary publication | Kapural, Yu, Doust et al., Anesthesiology 2015;123(4):851-860 [2] |
| 3 months | Back pain responders 84.5% HF10 vs 43.8% traditional SCS; leg pain responders 83.1% vs 55.5% (P < 0.001 for both); relative responder ratios 1.9 (95% CI 1.4-2.5) back, 1.5 (95% CI 1.2-1.9) leg [2] |
| 12 months | Superiority of HF10 over traditional SCS for back and leg pain sustained (P < 0.001) [2] |
| Paresthesia | HF10 subjects did not experience paresthesias; traditional SCS delivers its therapy with paresthesias by design [2] |
| 24 months | Kapural et al., Neurosurgery 2016;79(5):667-677: back pain responders 76.5% vs 49.3% (difference 27.2%, 95% CI 10.1-41.8; P < 0.001); leg pain responders 72.9% vs 49.3% (difference 23.6%, 95% CI 5.9-38.6; P = 0.003 for superiority); open access [3] |

## Regulatory outcome

| Field | Value and source scope |
| --- | --- |
| FDA pathway | PMA P130022, Senza SCS System delivering HF10 therapy; approved May 8, 2015 (company announcement dated that day; FDA PMA record) [4][5] |
| Label | Approved with labeling of superiority to traditional SCS for chronic back and leg pain (company announcement) [4] |
| Later iteration | The current Senza HFX iQ system (device sheet 155) was approved October 12, 2022 under supplement P130022/S044; the trial above used the original Senza configuration [5][6] |
