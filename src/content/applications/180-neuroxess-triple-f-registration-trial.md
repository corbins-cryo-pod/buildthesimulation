# NeuroXess Triple-F registration trial (NCT07720882)

The GCP registration trial of NeuroXess's [Triple-F fully implanted subdural BCI](/devices/180-neuroxess-triple-f-fully-implanted-subdural-bci/), filed under the generic name "implantable BCI hand motor function compensation system": a prospective, multicenter, single-arm target-value trial in tetraplegia after cervical spinal cord injury. Registry facts are from ClinicalTrials.gov (verified October 31, 2026). First-patient performance figures come from hospital and company disclosures in Chinese-language reporting, not a peer-reviewed paper, and are labeled as such.

## Study facts (NCT07720882)

| Field | Value and source scope |
| --- | --- |
| Registry | NCT07720882; prospective, multicenter, single-arm, no masking, target-value design [1] |
| Sponsor | NeuroXess Technology (Shanghai) Co., Ltd., with Huashan Hospital as collaborator; principal investigator Mao Ying [1][2] |
| Status and dates | Recruiting; actual start July 7, 2026; estimated primary completion January 30, 2027; estimated completion April 30, 2027 [1] |
| Enrollment | 32 (estimated) [1] |
| Sites | Registered site Huashan Hospital (Shanghai); company announcement describes 15 tertiary hospitals planned [1][2] |
| Population | Cervical spinal cord injury (C2 to C6), ASIA Impairment Scale grade A, B or C; severe hand impairment on the target arm (finger flexor strength below grade 3, defined ARAT score limits); injury at least 12 months before enrollment and stable at least 6 months; MMSE 22 or more and MoCA 26 or more; ages 18 to 65 [1] |
| Primary outcome | BCI-assisted ARAT response rate at 3 months post-operation: proportion whose BCI-assisted ARAT score minus the baseline unassisted score reaches the minimal clinically important difference [1] |
| Secondary outcomes | ARAT with and without BCI assistance (2, 3 and 6 months); five-task brain-control completion rate; six-direction target-selection success rate; Barthel Index ADL change; ISNCSCI change; number of well-functioning channels (implant day through 6 months); investigator-rated operability; subject satisfaction; monthly use time [1] |

## Evidence behind the trial

| Field | Value and source scope |
| --- | --- |
| First patient | Implanted October 30, 2025 at Huashan Hospital, a 28-year-old man paralysed for 8 years; first power-on 5 days after surgery; 5.2 bits per second on a 32 by 32 Webgrid test after 17 days of training, per the hospital director's December 5, 2025 talk (hospital disclosure via Shanghai Observer / Jiefang Daily) [3] |
| Channel reliability claim | 64 channels, with 100 percent conducting at 6 months in the first patient and at 4 months in the second, per a Xinmin Evening News report of company figures; the company's own news page does not state the channel count [4] |
| Regulatory context | Entered NMPA CMDE innovative-device special review, announced July 2026; NMPA published its BCI product classification guidance on June 30, 2026, treating implanted BCI as Class III, per the company; special review is not approval [2][4] |
| Evidence gap | No peer-reviewed clinical data for the Triple-F system identified as of October 31, 2026; the registration trial's primary readout (BCI-assisted ARAT response rate) is unproven until results are posted [1] |
