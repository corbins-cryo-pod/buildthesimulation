# NeuroPace RNS System pivotal trial and nine-year follow-up (NCT00264810)

The published human evidence behind the [NeuroPace RNS System](/devices/150-neuropace-rns-system/), the first FDA-approved brain-responsive neurostimulator: a Class I double-blind randomized pivotal trial and a nine-year prospective open-label follow-up. Figures are from the Neurology papers, the ClinicalTrials.gov record and the FDA summary of safety and effectiveness, verified November 6, 2026. Company brief: [NeuroPace, Inc.](/companies/52-neuropace-company-brief/).

## Pivotal randomized trial

| Field | Value and source scope |
| --- | --- |
| Registry | NCT00264810, "RNS System Pivotal-A Clinical Investigation"; sponsor NeuroPace; status completed; start December 2005, primary completion October 2009, completion May 2011 [3] |
| Design | Multicenter, double-blind, randomized, sham-controlled trial; 191 adults implanted with the responsive neurostimulator connected to depth or subdural leads at 1 or 2 predetermined seizure foci; randomized 1:1 one month after implantation to stimulation (treatment) or no stimulation (sham); 12-week blinded period followed by an 84-week open-label period in which all subjects received responsive stimulation [1] |
| Blinded-period result | Seizures reduced 37.9% in treatment (n=97) versus 17.3% in sham (n=94), p=0.012; no difference in adverse events between groups [1] |
| Open-label period | Seizure reduction sustained in the treatment group; the sham group improved significantly once stimulation began [1] |
| Quality of life and mood | Significant improvements in overall quality of life (p < 0.02); no deterioration in mood or neuropsychological function [1] |
| Evidence class | Class I evidence that responsive cortical stimulation reduces seizure frequency over 12 weeks in adults who failed 2 or more antiepileptic medications, with 3 or more seizures per month and 1 or 2 seizure foci [1] |

## Nine-year long-term treatment study

| Field | Value and source scope |
| --- | --- |
| Design | Prospective open-label long-term treatment (LTT) study enrolling participants from the 2-year feasibility and pivotal trials for up to 7 additional years [2] |
| Enrollment | 256 patients treated in the initial trials; 230 enrolled in the LTT (57 feasibility, 173 pivotal); 162 completed [2] |
| Efficacy at 9 years | Median seizure-frequency reduction 75% (p < 0.0001, Wilcoxon signed rank); responder rate 73%; 35% had a 90%-or-greater reduction [2] |
| Seizure freedom | 18.4% (47 of 256) experienced at least one year of seizure freedom; 62% of those (29 of 47) were seizure-free at last follow-up; average seizure-free period 3.2 years (range 1.04-9.6) [2] |
| Quality of life | Overall QOL and the epilepsy-targeted and cognitive domains of QOLIE-89 remained significantly improved (p < 0.05) [2] |
| Safety | No serious adverse events related to stimulation; implantation-related adverse events typical of other neurostimulation devices; SUDEP rate significantly lower than predefined comparators (p < 0.05) [2] |
| Evidence caveat | Open label without a concurrent control group; gains over time cannot be separated from placebo effects, regression to the mean or medication changes within this study [2] |

## Regulatory outcome

| Field | Value and source scope |
| --- | --- |
| Approval | FDA premarket approval P100026 on November 14, 2013, following a February 22, 2013 panel recommendation [4] |
| Indication | Adjunctive therapy to reduce seizure frequency in adults 18 and older with partial onset seizures, no more than 2 epileptogenic foci on diagnostic testing, refractory to two or more antiepileptic medications, averaging 3 or more disabling seizures per month [4] |
| Feasibility study | A 2-year feasibility study (n=65, IDE G010288) preceded the pivotal trial and fed the LTT [2][4] |
