# Separate Hydra and rat experiments

The JSSC paper uses the [PUF-addressed ME platform](/devices/124-puf-addressed-magnetoelectric-multisite-stimulator/) for two different biological demonstrations. It does not report multisite cardiac pacing or spinal-cord rehabilitation in an animal or person.

## Hydra synchronization

Two nearby Hydra vulgaris were stimulated with implants at varying heights powered by one coil. Stereotrodes attached to the devices delivered 3.5-V, 20-Hz, 1.2-ms biphasic trains. A transgenic GCaMP7b line visualized epithelial muscle activity, with greater-than-200% fluorescence increases and stimulus-aligned contractions.

This is synchronized muscle activation in two organisms, used as a model for coordinated stimulation. It is not biventricular pacing, a calcium-independent recording system or a clinical efficacy result. The two-Hydra illustration is not a complete study enrollment count.

## Acute rat nerve stimulation

A separate rat experiment used isoflurane anesthesia and surgical exposure of sciatic nerve. The implant was coated with Parylene C; the external transmitter was aligned at approximately 2 cm. Needle EMG electrodes in the foot and a shoulder reference recorded muscle activity.

With 1.2-ms, 3-Hz biphasic stimulation, the 3-V output produced stronger leg movement and plantar-muscle EMG than the 1-V output. The demonstrated motor recruitment is not recovery from injury, decoded intent or stable chronic operation.

The accessible in-vivo section describes this rat preparation but does not state a rat cohort size or repeated-trial denominator. Neither is inferred from a plotted trace or singular wording.

## Limits

Animal stimulation, two-device bench addressing and porcine-tissue power tests are separate evidence. The tissue test includes air gaps at longer separations; the 60-mm modeled power range is not a rat implantation depth. No human study, chronic survival, therapeutic pacing outcome or long-term implant reliability is reported here.
