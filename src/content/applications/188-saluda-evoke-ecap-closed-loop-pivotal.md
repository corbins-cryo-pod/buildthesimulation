# Saluda EVOKE pivotal trial (NCT02924129)

The completed pivotal trial of Saluda's Evoke system - an implanted spinal cord stimulator that records evoked compound action potentials (ECAPs) after every pulse and adjusts stimulation current in a closed loop to hold neural activation within each patient's therapeutic window - compared against the same hardware in fixed-output open-loop mode for chronic back and leg pain. Registry facts are from ClinicalTrials.gov (verified November 11, 2026). Results are peer reviewed through 36 months; the trial was the basis for FDA PMA P190002 (February 28, 2022).

## Study facts

| Field | Value and source scope |
| --- | --- |
| Registry | NCT02924129; interventional, randomized, parallel-arm, double-blind (participant and investigator) [1] |
| Official title | A Prospective, Multicenter, Randomized Double-blind Study Examining the Safety and Efficacy of Using the Evoke Spinal Cord Stimulator (SCS) System With Feedback to Treat Patients With Chronic Pain of the Trunk and/or Limbs [1] |
| Sponsor | Saluda Medical Americas, Inc. [1] |
| Status | Completed [1] |
| Sites | 13 specialist clinics, academic centers and hospitals in the USA [2] |
| Dates | Actual start January 27, 2017; actual completion September 9, 2022; patients enrolled February 21, 2017 to February 20, 2018 [1][2] |
| Enrollment | 134 (actual); 67 randomly assigned to each arm [1][2] |
| Arms | Evoke SCS with ECAP feedback (closed loop) vs Evoke SCS with conventional fixed-output stimulation (open loop), same system in both arms [1][2] |
| Population | Chronic, intractable back and leg pain (VAS at least 60 mm; ODI 41-80), refractory to conservative therapy, no prior spinal cord stimulation [2] |
| Primary outcome | Proportion of patients with a reduction of 50% or more in overall back and leg pain with no increase in pain medications; non-inferiority (10% margin) then superiority, intention-to-treat, at 3 months (primary analysis) and 12 months (prespecified) after permanent implant [1][2] |
| Registry results | No results posted to ClinicalTrials.gov as of November 11, 2026 [1] |

## Results (peer reviewed)

| Field | Value and source scope |
| --- | --- |
| Primary publication | Mekhail, Levy, Deer et al. (Evoke Study Group), The Lancet Neurology 2020;19(2):123-134 [2] |
| 3 months | Composite primary outcome achieved by 51 of 62 (82.3%) closed-loop vs 38 of 63 (60.3%) open-loop patients; difference 21.9% (95% CI 6.6-37.3), p=0.0052 [2] |
| 12 months | 49 of 59 (83.1%) closed-loop vs 36 of 59 (61.0%) open-loop; difference 22.0% (95% CI 6.3-37.7), p=0.0060 [2] |
| Safety | No difference in safety profiles between arms; most frequent study-related adverse events: lead migration 9 patients (7%), IPG pocket pain 5 (4%), muscle spasm or cramp 3 (2%) [2] |
| 24 months | Mekhail et al., JAMA Neurology 2022: overall-pain responders 53 of 67 (79.1%) closed-loop vs 36 of 67 (53.7%) open-loop; difference 25.4% (95% CI 10.0-40.8), p=0.001; opioid reduction or elimination in parallel; no safety difference [3] |
| 36 months | Mekhail et al., Regional Anesthesia and Pain Medicine 2024: at least 50% pain reduction in 77.6% closed-loop vs 49.3% open-loop (difference 28.4%, 95% CI 12.8-43.9, p<0.001); at least 80% reduction 49.3% vs 31.3% (p=0.032); no explants for loss of efficacy in the closed-loop arm [4] |
| Mechanistic note | The closed-loop arm showed greater and more consistent spinal cord activation on objective ECAP measurement, aligning clinical benefit with the putative mechanism of action [2][3][4] |
